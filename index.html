What we do

From the flight to data you can use

Altamira Drones is a Madrid-based technical drone services company. We capture the data, process it and deliver it as something you can act on: a report that pinpoints the problem, a model you can measure from the office, a drawing you can take to site.

We work for contractors, developers, architects, engineering firms, public bodies and farms. Each service is described below with what it includes, what we deliver and when it makes sense.

How we work

  • We handle the permits and coordination for every flight, including in Madrid's controlled airspace.
  • Planned, repeatable flights: same route and altitude, so results can be compared over time.
  • Deliverables in standard formats and in a web viewer you can open on your phone.

01

Exterior thermal imaging of building envelopes

Passivhaus and high-efficiency buildings

From inside, thermography is done room by room: the picture is fragmented, depends on the furniture and on being able to enter every home. From outside, the drone sees the whole envelope in one continuous view, including upper floors, slab edges, roof junctions and parapets that are barely visible from the ground.

In a Passivhaus the defects are small and localised, which is exactly why reading the whole façade at once matters: a slab edge without a thermal break or a section of badly fitted insulation shows up as a line or a patch warmer than the rest of the wall.

What's included
  • Thermal bridges at slabs, columns, lintels and window frames
  • Gaps or missing insulation in façades and roofs
  • Air leaks through the envelope, using the combined method
Deliverable
Thermal mosaic of every façade and the roof, numbered anomalies and report
When
November to March, at dawn or at night

Combined method with the blower door test

Exterior thermography during the pressurisation test

A blower door test measures how much air leaks out of a building, but not where. If the test is run under pressurisation while we fly, the warmer indoor air escapes through every leak and heats the façade right at that point. From outside, each leak shows up as a localised warm spot.

It uses a visit that is already scheduled, nobody has to enter the homes, and the result is a façade drawing with the leaks marked, ready to be fixed before the test is repeated.

02

ETICS quality control

External insulation: during construction and at handover

ETICS defects almost always originate during installation: an open joint between boards, anchors without their insulating cap, a poorly detailed junction with a window or the base. Once the render is applied they can no longer be seen, and they show up in the blower door test, when fixing them costs far more.

A thermal flight before the finish checks every panel in full: the anchors form a grid of dots and any uninsulated joint or gap breaks it. Once the system is finished, the same inspection finds debonding, moisture inside the system and cracks tracing the board joints.

What's included
  • Thermal flight of every panel before rendering
  • Handover of the finished system: debonding, moisture and cracks
  • In refurbishment, measurement of façades and openings for costing
Deliverable
Elevations with every defect located, to fix before the panel is closed
When
Before the finish, with a temperature difference; debonding, as the façade cools after sunshine
03

Moisture in flat roofs

Find the water before opening up the roof

Water trapped under the waterproofing stores the day's heat. At dusk, wet areas cool more slowly than dry ones and show up in the thermal image as irregular patches, usually around drains, parapets and service penetrations.

With a moisture map, repairs are targeted: the roof is opened only where needed. It is useful at handover, for after-sales and for settling warranty claims with an objective record.

What's included
  • Wet areas outlined on the roof plan
  • Affected area in m²
  • Recommended inspection points
When
At dusk, after a sunny day, with the roof surface dry
Note
Thermal imaging shows where the water is, not always where it gets in: confirmed with a moisture meter or core
04

Thermal inspection of solar panels

Commissioning and periodic checks

A module or string that stops producing gives no warning: it shows up on the bill months later. With full sun and the system running, each type of fault leaves a recognisable thermal pattern: a hot cell, a third of a module warmer because of a failed bypass diode, a whole row at a different temperature because a string is disconnected.

Doing it at building handover confirms the system works before it passes to the owner, and repeating it during the warranty period avoids disputes about when a defect appeared.

What's included
  • Hot spots, broken modules and failed bypass diodes
  • Disconnected strings, soiling and shading
  • Priority rating for every affected module
Deliverable
Plan of the array with each module identified, fault type and priority
When
Middle of the day in clear weather, all year round
05

Visual inspection of façades and roofs

Defects surveyed without scaffolding or lifts

Cracks, spalling, displaced tiles, poorly finished flashings, visible damp. The drone gets close to any point of the façade or roof and records it in millimetre detail, with no access equipment and no risk to anyone.

Each defect is photographed, numbered and located on the orthophoto or elevation, so the report works equally well for pricing repairs, for after-sales and for statutory building inspections.

What's included
  • Overall flight and close-up shots of each defect
  • Roof orthophoto or façade elevation
  • Photographic report by zone, numbered and georeferenced
Detail
Down to 2 mm per pixel in close-up shots
Useful for
After-sales, repairs and building inspections
06

Photogrammetry and 3D modelling

Measurable models, orthophotos and point clouds

From hundreds of overlapping photographs we reconstruct the real geometry of a building or a site: a textured 3D model on which distances, areas and volumes can be measured, an undistorted orthophoto and a dense point cloud from which plans, elevations and sections are drawn.

It is the basis for almost everything else: surveying an existing building before refurbishment, as-built documentation, and comparing two stages of the same project.

What's included
  • Textured 3D model and web viewer to measure in the browser
  • Orthophoto and point cloud
  • Plans, elevations and sections from the cloud
Formats
OBJ, LAS / LAZ, GeoTIFF and DXF, compatible with CAD, BIM and GIS
Accuracy
Centimetre-level, with ground control points
07

Surveying and earthwork volumes

Cut, fill and stockpiles

The digital terrain model produced by the flight allows volumes to be calculated with centimetre accuracy in a fraction of the time of a walking survey, and without anyone having to walk among the machinery.

Comparing this month's flight with the previous one, or with the design surface, gives the volume moved in the period: an objective figure to certify to the client and to check what subcontractors certify.

What's included
  • Digital terrain model and contour lines
  • Cut, fill and stockpile volumes
  • Comparison between dates or against the design
Deliverable
Volume report, DTM (GeoTIFF) and contours (DXF)
Frequency
Monthly, at each valuation
08

Construction: the moments worth documenting

Not a daily flight, but the flights that leave a record

On a large site, the day-to-day is already covered by the site teams. What a photo does not give you is a measurable, dated and georeferenced record at the moments when that data is worth money.

Before starting, a pre-construction condition survey of neighbouring buildings, roads and pavements: the best defence against third-party claims. Before covering up, buried services, slab reinforcement or waterproofing that will never be seen again. And at the end, as-built documentation for the owner, the archive and maintenance.

What's included
  • Pre-construction condition survey
  • Milestone flights before covering up
  • As-built aerial documentation
Deliverable
Measurable 3D model and orthophoto of each milestone, in a web viewer
Frequency
By milestone, at the site manager's request
09

Processing your own teams' flights

The frequency a large site needs, without a pilot every day

Many site foremen already take photos with their own drone, but loose photos cannot be measured. The idea is to make use of those flights: we load an automatic route, prepared for each site, onto their drone. It starts with one tap and takes a few minutes.

They fly whenever they need to, and we process the images and return a measurable 3D model in a web viewer. The site team gets the record as often as they want, and we provide the processing and data quality control.

What's included
  • Flight route design for each site
  • Short training for site foremen
  • Processing, web viewer and archive of every flight
Requirement
A drone that supports automatic missions; we check it site by site
Turnaround
Model available 24–48 h after the flight
10

Green areas: inventory and irrigation efficiency

Maintenance of parks, gardens and residential estates

Maintaining a green area well requires knowing what is there, where it is and where there is too much or too little water. From the air we produce a georeferenced inventory of trees and of lawn, shrub and paved areas, the basis of any maintenance contract.

The thermal camera finds irrigation leaks and waterlogged areas and detects water stress: a canopy warmer than those around it is a plant that is not transpiring. With these maps, irrigation sectors and schedules can be adjusted.

What's included
  • Inventory of trees and surfaces
  • Irrigation leaks and waterlogged areas
  • Vigour and water-stress map
Deliverable
GIS inventory layer and report with irrigation improvements
When
Spring and summer

12

Agriculture and livestock

Aerial data for decisions in the field

Our background is in agricultural engineering. With multispectral and thermal cameras we measure crop condition, outline zones that behave differently and turn that variability into management maps: where to spray, where to check irrigation, where to sample.

For livestock, the drone makes it possible to supervise large areas, check fences, water points and facilities, and support head counts without covering the farm on foot.

Agriculture
  • Spectral indices (NDVI, SAVI and others) and vigour maps
  • Prescription maps for variable-rate spraying and fertilising
  • Field and management-zone mapping for sampling
  • Early detection of stress and crop issues
  • Irrigation support: zones with anomalous behaviour
  • Aerial application within the regulatory framework
Livestock
  • Locating and counting animals
  • Pasture and land-use monitoring
  • Inspection of fences, water points and facilities
  • Fast monitoring of large areas
